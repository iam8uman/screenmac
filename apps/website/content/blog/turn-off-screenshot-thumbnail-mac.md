To turn off the screenshot thumbnail on a Mac, press **Shift-Command-5**, click **Options**, and untick **Show Floating Thumbnail**. From then on, screenshots and recordings save immediately with no preview floating in the corner. Turn it back on the same way if you miss the quick edit button.

## How do I turn off the floating thumbnail?

1. Press <kbd>Shift</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>5</kbd>. The Screenshot toolbar appears.
2. Click **Options**.
3. Click **Show Floating Thumbnail** to remove its tick.
4. Press **Esc** to close the toolbar.

The setting applies to all screenshot shortcuts, including <kbd>Shift</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>3</kbd> and <kbd>4</kbd>, and to screen recordings.

## What does the floating thumbnail do?

Apple's guide describes a preview that appears in the bottom-right corner for a few seconds after each capture. While it is showing you can:

- **Click it** to open the screenshot to mark up, trim (for recordings) or share.
- **Drag it** into a document, an email, a note or a Finder window to put the file there.
- **Swipe it to the right** to save and dismiss it straight away.
- **Hold the pointer over it** to keep it on screen until you decide.

Control-clicking the thumbnail shows more choices, including saving to the clipboard instead.

## Why would I turn it off?

| Annoyance | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| The file is not there yet | With the thumbnail on, the screenshot saves when the thumbnail goes away, a few seconds later |
| It gets in the next screenshot | Taking several captures in a row can catch the previous thumbnail in the corner |
| It covers part of the screen | It sits over whatever is in the bottom-right corner |
| You never edit right away | You save files and open them later anyway |

If any of these bother you more than the thumbnail helps, turn it off. If only the delay bothers you, swipe the thumbnail to the right instead, which saves at once.

## What do I lose by turning it off?

You lose the quick markup window and the drag-and-drop shortcut. Neither is hard to replace:

- **To mark up:** double-click the saved file to open it in Preview, then click **Show Markup Toolbar**. See [how to mark up a screenshot in Preview](/blog/markup-screenshot-preview-mac).
- **To trim a recording:** open it in QuickTime Player and choose **Edit → Trim**. See [how to trim a screen recording](/blog/trim-screen-recording-mac).
- **To paste a screenshot:** add <kbd>Control</kbd> to the shortcut to copy it to the clipboard instead of dragging a thumbnail. See [how to copy a screenshot to the clipboard](/blog/copy-screenshot-to-clipboard-mac).

## Can I keep a preview but control it better?

Capture apps usually have their own preview. Screenmac shows a **Quick Access** preview after each capture from which you copy, save or drag the image, or open its editor with arrows, numbered steps, blur and redaction. It is separate from Apple's thumbnail, so you can turn Apple's off and still have a preview for captures taken with Screenmac's own shortcuts. [Download Screenmac](/download) if that suits you, or try the editor first in your browser at [/editor](/editor).

## What else can I set in Options?

The same menu holds the other screenshot settings:

- **Save to:** desktop, a folder, the clipboard or an app. See [changing where screenshots are saved](/blog/change-screenshot-location-mac).
- **Timer:** 5 or 10 seconds for menus and hover states.
- **Show Mouse Pointer:** include the pointer in captures.
- **Remember Last Selection:** reuse the last area for the next capture.
