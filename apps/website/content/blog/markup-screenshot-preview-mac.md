To mark up a screenshot on a Mac, open it in **Preview** and click **Show Markup Toolbar**, or click the floating thumbnail right after you take it. The toolbar has **Shapes** (including arrows and boxes), **Text**, **Highlight**, a **Zoom** lens, **Sketch** and **Draw**, plus **Crop**, colors and line styles. When you are done, export a PNG or JPG so the markup is flattened into the image. Preview cannot blur or pixelate.

## How do I open the Markup toolbar?

- **Right after a capture:** click the thumbnail that floats in the corner. A Markup window opens; click **Done** to save.
- **For a saved screenshot:** double-click it to open it in Preview, then click the **Show Markup Toolbar** button in the toolbar.

## What does each Markup tool do?

Apple's Preview guide lists these tools for images:

| Tool | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Rectangular, Elliptical, Lasso, Smart Lasso Selection | Select an area to copy, delete or crop |
| Instant Alpha | Remove a background |
| Sketch | Draw a shape in one stroke; it snaps to a standard shape if it matches one |
| Draw | Pressure-sensitive line on Macs with a Force Touch trackpad |
| Shapes | Rectangles, ovals, lines, arrows and more; blue handles resize, green handles reshape |
| Highlight | A see-through highlight shape |
| Zoom | A magnifying lens; green handle sets zoom, blue handle sets size |
| Text | A text box you can drag anywhere |
| Sign | Add a saved signature |
| Shape Style, Border Color, Fill Color, Text Style | Line thickness, shadows, colors and fonts |
| Adjust Color, Adjust Size | Exposure and contrast; image dimensions |
| Rotate, Crop | Turn the image or trim it to a selection |
| Image Description | Alt text that screen readers can read |

## How do I add an arrow and a label?

1. Click **Shapes** and choose the arrow.
2. Drag the arrow so its head points at the thing you are explaining.
3. Use **Shape Style** to make the line thicker, and **Border Color** to pick a color that stands out.
4. Click **Text**, type your label and drag it next to the arrow.
5. Use **Text Style** to set the font size and color.

Keep it to one or two arrows per screenshot. More than that and readers cannot tell what matters.

## How do I hide private information in Preview?

Preview has no blur, so:

1. Click **Shapes** and choose a rectangle.
2. Drag it over the private area.
3. Set **Fill Color** and **Border Color** to a solid color.
4. Choose **File → Export** and save a PNG. The export is flat, so the rectangle cannot be moved off.

Do not use **Highlight** to hide text; it is designed to be see-through. For blur, pixelate and one-click redaction, the free [browser screenshot editor](/editor) does it in your browser, and our guide on [blurring part of a screenshot](/blog/blur-part-of-screenshot-mac) compares the options.

## How do I crop in Preview?

Choose **Rectangular Selection**, drag over the part you want to keep, then click **Crop**. To see the exact pixel size of the selection, choose **Tools → Show Inspector** and open the crop pane. Our guide on [cropping a screenshot](/blog/crop-screenshot-mac) has more.

## Where does Preview fall short?

| You need | Preview | Alternative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blur or pixelate | No | The [browser editor](/editor) or a capture app |
| Numbered step markers | No, type numbers in text boxes | Editors with a counter tool; see [numbered steps](/blog/numbered-steps-screenshot) |
| Consistent arrow style every time | Set by hand each time | Capture apps remember styles |
| Edit right after every capture | Through the thumbnail | Capture apps open their editor automatically |

If you annotate most of your screenshots, a capture app with a built-in editor saves a lot of clicks. [Screenmac](/download) is free and opens an editor with arrows, shapes, text, numbered steps, highlighter, crop, blur, pixelate and redact after each capture, with redactions flattened into the exported pixels.
