To trim a screen recording on a Mac, open it in **QuickTime Player**, choose **Edit → Trim**, drag the **yellow handles** so only the part you want to keep is inside them, and click **Trim**. Then choose **File → Save** (or Export As for a smaller copy). If you have just stopped recording, you can also click the floating thumbnail and trim there before the file is saved.

Both methods are built in and free. Here are the steps, plus how to cut a section out of the middle.

## How do I trim a recording right after stopping it?

When you stop a recording made with <kbd>Shift</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>5</kbd>, a thumbnail floats in the corner of the screen for a few seconds. Apple's guide notes you can click it to mark up, trim or share.

1. Click the thumbnail before it disappears.
2. In the preview window, click the **Trim** button in the toolbar.
3. Drag the yellow handles at each end of the timeline to where the video should start and stop.
4. Click **Trim**, then **Done**.

The trimmed version is what gets saved. If the thumbnail has already gone, use QuickTime as below.

## How do I trim a screen recording in QuickTime Player?

1. Open the recording in **QuickTime Player**. Double-clicking a `.mov` file usually does this.
2. Choose **Edit → Trim**. A trimming bar with yellow handles appears under the video.
3. Drag the left handle to the first moment you want to keep and the right handle to the last.
4. For precision, **click and hold a handle**. Apple's guide says this shows individual frames so you can pick the exact one.
5. Click **Trim**. Everything outside the handles is removed.
6. Choose **File → Save** to keep the change, or **File → Export As** to save a separate copy.

Press the space bar to play the selection before you click Trim, so you can check you have not cut off the end of a sentence.

## How do I cut out a section in the middle?

Trim keeps one continuous part. To remove a mistake from the middle, split the recording into clips first:

1. Move the playhead to where the unwanted part begins.
2. Choose **Edit → Split Clip**. The video splits into two clips.
3. Move the playhead to where the unwanted part ends and split again.
4. Click the middle clip to select it and press **Delete**.
5. Click **Done**, then save.

To fine-tune one clip, choose **View → Show Clips**, double-click the clip, and trim it with the same yellow handles.

## How do I make the trimmed file smaller?

Trimming removes footage, so the file usually shrinks. If it is still too big for email or chat, export a lower resolution:

| QuickTime export | What you get |
| --- | --- |
| 4K or 1080p | .mov, with a choice of HEVC (smaller) or H.264 (more compatible) |
| 720p | .mov using H.264, up to 1280 × 720 |
| 480p | .mov using H.264, up to 640 × 480 |
| Audio Only | An audio file with an AAC track |

Choose **File → Export As** and pick a resolution. Options larger than the recording are dimmed. Full details are in [how to make a Mac screen recording smaller](/blog/reduce-screen-recording-file-size-mac), and the [screen recording file size calculator](/tools/screen-recording-file-size-calculator) shows how length and bitrate drive size, so you can see how much a trim will save.

## When should I use a video editor instead?

QuickTime covers trimming, splitting, deleting clips, rotating and joining clips. Use a full editor such as iMovie when you need:

- Titles, captions or callouts on screen.
- Zooming in on part of the screen.
- Music or a separate voice-over track.
- Transitions between scenes.

For most screen recordings that go to a colleague or into a ticket, a clean trim is all you need.

## How do I avoid trimming in the first place?

- Use **Options → Timer** in the Screenshot toolbar so recording starts a few seconds after you click Record, giving you time to get ready.
- Record a **selected portion** rather than the whole screen so you do not catch yourself moving windows into place.
- Stop with <kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>Esc</kbd> instead of reaching for the menu bar, so the end is clean.
