To screen record with your voice on a Mac, press **Shift-Command-5**, click **Options**, and choose a microphone under **Microphone**, such as the built-in mic or a headset. Then choose what to record, click **Record**, and talk as you go. In QuickTime Player, the same choice is in the menu next to the Record button. Wear headphones if you also play sound, so the mic does not pick it up.

## How do I choose a microphone for recording?

**In the Screenshot toolbar:**

1. Press <kbd>Shift</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>5</kbd>.
2. Click **Options**.
3. Under **Microphone**, choose the mic you want. **None** records no sound.
4. Choose **Record Entire Screen**, **Record Selected Window** (macOS Tahoe 26 or later) or **Record Selected Portion**, then click **Record**.

**In QuickTime Player:**

1. Choose **File → New Screen Recording**.
2. If a Screen Recording window appears, click the arrow next to **Record** and choose a microphone. QuickTime shows a volume slider so you can monitor the audio as you record.
3. Click **Record**.

The choice is remembered for next time, so check it before an important recording.

## Why is my voice missing from the recording?

| Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Microphone set to None | Options → Microphone, pick your mic |
| Wrong mic selected | A headset may be connected but not chosen; pick it explicitly |
| App lacks microphone permission | System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone, turn the app on |
| Bluetooth headset switching modes | Try the built-in mic or a wired mic to rule it out |
| Mic muted in hardware | Some headsets and USB mics have a mute switch |

The built-in tools are Apple's own and normally have access. Third-party recorders need you to allow them under **Microphone** in Privacy & Security, separately from screen recording permission.

## How do I avoid echo and background noise?

- **Use headphones.** Apple's guide suggests lowering the volume or using headphones with a microphone to avoid feedback.
- **Choose the closest mic.** A headset or desk mic near your mouth beats the laptop's built-in mic across the room.
- **Turn on Do Not Disturb** so notification sounds do not interrupt.
- **Close noisy apps** and anything with alerts.
- **Record a ten-second test** and listen back with headphones before the real take.

## Which microphone should I use?

| Microphone | Good for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Built-in MacBook mic | Quick clips, internal updates | Picks up typing and room echo |
| Wired headset | Calls and tutorials with sound playing | Cable noise if it rubs on clothes |
| Bluetooth headphones with a mic | Convenience | Some headsets drop to lower audio quality when the mic is in use |
| USB desk microphone | Courses, product demos | Needs to sit close to your mouth to beat room noise |

Whatever you use, keep it at the same distance through the recording, and record in a soft-furnished room if you can: curtains, carpets and sofas absorb echo better than bare walls and desks.

## Can I record my voice and the Mac's sound together?

On **macOS 27 Golden Gate or later**, Apple lists a **System audio** option in the Screenshot toolbar's Options, which you can use alongside a microphone. On earlier versions, the built-in recorder captures only the microphone. Our guide to [screen recording with internal audio](/blog/record-screen-with-audio-mac) covers the options for each version.

## How do I sound clear in a tutorial?

- **Write a short outline.** Three or four points keep you from rambling.
- **Speak a little slower than normal.** Viewers are also watching the screen.
- **Pause before each click.** It gives viewers time to see where you are going and makes trimming easier.
- **Re-record short sections** rather than living with a stumble; separate short clips are easier to redo.
