The best free screen recorder for most Mac users in 2027 is the one already installed: press **Shift-Command-5** to record the whole screen, a selected part or (on macOS Tahoe 26 or later) a single window, with your microphone and, on macOS 27 Golden Gate, system audio. For streaming, webcam overlays and audio mixing, **OBS Studio** is free and open source. For GIFs, **Kap**.

For recordings alongside annotated, redacted screenshots that never leave your Mac, **Screenmac**. **Loom's** free plan suits quick shared video messages if its limits fit.

Features below come from Apple's documentation and each app's own site, checked when this guide was written.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Screenshot toolbar | Screen, portion, window (Tahoe 26+) | Mic; system audio on macOS 27+ | .mov | No editing beyond trim | None |
| QuickTime Player | Screen or portion | Mic | .mov | Basic trim and split | None |

## 1. The built-in Screenshot toolbar: best for most people

Press <kbd>Shift</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>5</kbd> and choose **Record Entire Screen**, **Record Selected Window** (macOS Tahoe 26 or later) or **Record Selected Portion**. Options add a microphone, a timer, **Show Mouse Clicks**, the save location and, on macOS 27 Golden Gate or later, **System audio**. Stop with <kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>Esc</kbd>, and click the thumbnail to trim.

**Best for:** quick walkthroughs and bug videos. **Missing:** a webcam overlay and editing tools. Steps are in [how to record your screen on a Mac](/blog/record-screen-mac).

## 2. QuickTime Player: best for simple edits afterwards

QuickTime's **File → New Screen Recording** uses the same recorder, and QuickTime opens the recording when you stop. Its editing tools trim, split, rearrange and rotate clips, and **File → Export As** saves at 4K, 1080p, 720p or 480p. Its **File → New Movie Recording** also records from a connected iPhone or iPad.

**Best for:** recording, then trimming and exporting a smaller copy in one app. See [how to trim a screen recording](/blog/trim-screen-recording-mac).

## 3. OBS Studio: best for streaming and complex recordings

OBS calls itself free and open-source software for video recording and live streaming. You build scenes from window captures, images, text, webcams and capture cards, switch between them with transitions, and mix audio with filters such as noise suppression and gain. It runs on macOS 13 or newer, on Apple silicon and Intel.

**Best for:** streamers, course creators and anyone who wants a webcam bubble for free. **Trade-off:** it takes time to set up compared with one-shortcut recorders.

## 4. Kap: best for quick GIFs

Kap is an open-source recorder under the MIT license that exports **GIF, MP4, WebM and APNG**, with optional audio, click highlighting and trimming. Its site lists macOS 12 or later, with builds for Apple silicon and Intel. It is built with web technology rather than as a native app.

**Best for:** short clips and GIFs for pull requests, docs and chat.

## 5. Screenmac: best for recordings alongside screenshots

Screenmac is made by the team behind this site. It is a native macOS app for Sonoma (14) or later that records a display, an area or a window to **MP4 with an optional microphone**, and takes screenshots that open in an editor with arrows, numbered steps and redaction flattened into the pixels. There is no account and no upload feature; recordings and history stay on your Mac.

It does not have a webcam overlay or video editing, and it is a free beta whose builds are not notarized by Apple yet. **Best for:** people who explain things with a mix of short videos and marked-up screenshots. [Download Screenmac](/download).

## 6. Loom's free plan: best for shared video messages

Loom's pricing page lists the **Starter** plan at $0 with up to **25 videos per person**, **5-minute** recordings and quality up to **720p**. Recordings are shared as links through Loom. Paid plans start at $18 per user per month.

**Best for:** async updates to teammates. **Trade-off:** videos live on Loom's service. If that is a problem, see [Loom alternatives that record locally](/blog/loom-alternative-mac).
