To add arrows, boxes and text to a screenshot on a Mac, click the **floating thumbnail** right after capturing (or open the file in **Preview** and click **Show Markup Toolbar**), choose **Shapes** for arrows and rectangles and **Text** for labels, then save. For faster work, the free [browser screenshot editor](/editor) has one-key tools: <kbd>A</kbd> for arrows, <kbd>R</kbd> for rectangles, <kbd>T</kbd> for text and <kbd>H</kbd> for the highlighter, and exports a PNG.

## How do I annotate a screenshot with Markup?

1. Take the screenshot and click the thumbnail that appears in the corner. For an older screenshot, open it in Preview and click **Show Markup Toolbar**.
2. Click **Shapes** and choose an arrow, rectangle or oval. Drag it into place; blue handles resize it.
3. Click **Text** to add a label, then drag the text box next to your arrow.
4. Use **Shape Style**, **Border Color** and **Text Style** to set line thickness, colors and font.
5. Click **Done** (thumbnail) or save the file (Preview).

Markup is built in and good enough for occasional use. It is slower for frequent use because styles need setting each time, and it has no blur or numbered steps. All of Preview's tools are listed in [how to mark up a screenshot in Preview](/blog/markup-screenshot-preview-mac).

## How do I annotate faster in the browser editor?

1. Open the [screenshot editor](/editor), then open, drop or paste your screenshot (<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>V</kbd>).
2. Press a key to pick a tool:

| Key | Tool |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>A</kbd> | Arrow |
| <kbd>L</kbd> | Line |
| <kbd>R</kbd> | Rectangle |
| <kbd>F</kbd> | Filled rectangle |
| <kbd>O</kbd> | Ellipse |
| <kbd>T</kbd> | Text |
| <kbd>N</kbd> | Numbered step (counter) |
| <kbd>H</kbd> | Highlighter |
| <kbd>P</kbd> | Pencil |
| <kbd>C</kbd> | Crop |
| <kbd>B</kbd>, <kbd>X</kbd>, <kbd>D</kbd> | Blur, pixelate, redact |
| <kbd>V</kbd> | Select, move and resize |

3. Drag on the image to draw. Hold Shift while drawing lines and arrows to snap the angle.
4. Click **Export PNG** or copy the result to the clipboard.

The editor runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded. [Screenmac](/download), the free Mac app, opens the same kind of editor after every capture taken with its shortcuts.

## How do I make annotations people actually read?

- **One message per screenshot.** If you need three arrows, you probably need three screenshots or numbered steps.
- **Box the target, point with an arrow.** A rectangle shows exactly what you mean; an arrow guides the eye from your label to it.
- **Short labels.** Three to six words beat a sentence. Put explanations in the message that goes with the image.
- **One color.** A single bright color for every annotation looks deliberate and is easy to follow.
- **Leave space.** Crop so there is room for labels in an empty area, not on top of the content.
- **Check at actual size.** Annotations that look bold on a Retina screen can look thin when the image is shown smaller.

## Which tool should I use?

| You annotate | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A few times a month | Markup from the thumbnail |
| Away from your own Mac | The browser editor |
| Several times a day | A capture app with an editor, such as Screenmac, CleanShot X or Shottr |
| With private details to hide | An editor with real blur and redaction |
