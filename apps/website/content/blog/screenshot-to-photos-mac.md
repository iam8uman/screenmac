Mac screenshots do **not** go into the Photos app on their own; macOS saves them as files on the desktop or in the folder chosen under **Save to**. To put them in Photos, open **Photos**, choose **File → Import**, select the screenshots and import them, or simply **drag the files** from Finder onto the Photos window or the Photos icon in the Dock. By default Photos copies them into its library.

## How do I import screenshots into Photos?

1. Open **Photos**.
2. Choose **File → Import**.
3. Find your screenshots (on the desktop by default) and select them. Hold <kbd>Command</kbd> to pick several.
4. Click **Review for Import**, then import the ones you want.

Or drag them in: select the screenshots in Finder and drag them onto the Photos window, or onto the Photos icon in the Dock. Apple's Photos guide describes both methods.

## Why would I want screenshots in Photos?

| Reason | Why Photos helps |
| --- | --- |
| You want them on your iPhone | With iCloud Photos on, the library syncs across your devices |
| You keep receipts, tickets or confirmations | Photos search and albums make them easy to find later |
| You copy text from them | Live Text works in Photos: select the text and choose Copy Text |
| You want albums | Group screenshots of a trip, a project or a purchase |

For working files, such as screenshots for a bug report or documentation, a folder is usually better than Photos: you can name, sort and attach them more easily.

## What should I check before importing?

**iCloud sync.** If iCloud Photos is on, everything you import is uploaded to iCloud and appears on your other devices. Screenshots of passwords, bank pages or private messages go with it. Import only what you want everywhere.

**Private details.** If the screenshot shows information you would not want on every device, redact it first. The free [screenshot editor](/editor) blurs or blocks out parts of the image in your browser and exports a new PNG; import that instead.

**Duplicates.** Photos copies the file into its library by default. Once the import is done, you can delete the original from the desktop to avoid two copies.

## Can I make screenshots save straight into Photos?

The Screenshot app's **Save to** list offers the desktop, Documents, the clipboard, Mail, Messages, Preview and any folder, but Photos is not one of the documented choices. A practical setup is:

1. Set **Save to** to a dedicated folder, such as **Screenshots** in Pictures. See [how to change where screenshots are saved](/blog/change-screenshot-location-mac).
2. Every so often, open Photos, choose **File → Import**, and import the screenshots you want to keep.
3. Delete the rest.

## What about screenshots from my iPhone?

Screenshots taken on an iPhone do go into Photos, and with iCloud Photos on they appear in Photos on your Mac too. From there you can drag one out to Finder to get a file, or open it to mark it up.

## How do I keep screenshots under control?

Screenshots pile up fast. A dedicated folder plus occasional cleanup works better than letting them collect on the desktop. Copying straight to the clipboard (add <kbd>Control</kbd> to the shortcut) for screenshots you only need once helps too. [Screenmac](/download) also keeps a local capture history, so you can find past captures without saving every one as a file.
