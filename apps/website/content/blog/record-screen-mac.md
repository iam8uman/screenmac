To record your screen on a Mac, press **Shift-Command-5**, choose **Record Entire Screen** or **Record Selected Portion** in the toolbar, then click **Record**. Stop with the **Stop** button in the menu bar or **Command-Control-Esc**. The recording saves to your desktop as a .mov file. Pick a microphone under **Options** first if you want your voice.

On macOS Tahoe 26 or later you can also record a single window, and QuickTime Player offers the same recorder with a slightly different window.

## How do I record the whole screen?

1. Press <kbd>Shift</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>5</kbd>. The Screenshot toolbar appears.
2. Click **Record Entire Screen**. With more than one display, click the screen you want to record.
3. Click **Options** if you want to set a microphone, a timer or the save location (see below).
4. Click **Record**.
5. When you are done, click the **Stop** button in the menu bar, or press <kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>Esc</kbd>.

A thumbnail appears in the corner. Click it to trim or share the recording, or leave it and it saves.

## How do I record only part of the screen?

1. Press <kbd>Shift</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>5</kbd>.
2. Click **Record Selected Portion**.
3. Drag the frame to move it, or drag its edges to resize it, until it covers what you want.
4. Click **Record**, and stop as above.

Recording a smaller area makes the file smaller and keeps viewers' eyes on what matters. Turn on **Remember Last Selection** in Options to reuse the same frame next time.

## How do I record a single window?

On **macOS Tahoe 26 or later**, the toolbar has **Record Selected Window**. Choose it, click **Record**, then click the window you want. Only that window is recorded.

On earlier versions, use Record Selected Portion and draw the frame around the window. Keep the window still while you record, because the frame does not follow it.

## What can I set in Options before recording?

| Option | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Save to | Desktop by default, or Documents, another folder or an app |
| Timer | Waits 5 or 10 seconds after you click Record |
| Microphone | Records your voice from the chosen mic; None for silence |
| System audio | Records sound from apps on your Mac (macOS 27 Golden Gate or later) |
| Show Mouse Clicks | Draws a black circle around the pointer when you click |
| Remember Last Selection | Reuses your last frame for portion recordings |
| Capture format | SDR (most compatible, H.264) or HDR (HEVC) on supported Macs with macOS Tahoe 26 or later |

If you want your Mac's own sound in the recording, read [how to screen record with internal audio](/blog/record-screen-with-audio-mac), which explains what each macOS version allows. If you hear an echo while recording your voice, Apple suggests lowering the volume or using headphones.

## How do I record with QuickTime Player instead?

1. Open **QuickTime Player** from Applications.
2. Choose **File → New Screen Recording**, or press <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>N</kbd>.
3. If the Screenshot toolbar appears, it is the same recorder as above. If a Screen Recording window appears instead, click the arrow next to **Record** to choose a microphone and **Show Mouse Clicks in Recording**.
4. Click **Record**, then click anywhere to record the whole screen, or drag to select an area and click **Start Recording**.
5. Stop from the menu bar or with <kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>Esc</kbd>.

QuickTime opens the recording when you stop, ready to play, trim or save.

## What should I do after recording?

- **Trim the start and end.** Click the thumbnail, or open the file in QuickTime and choose **Edit → Trim**. Our guide to [trimming a screen recording](/blog/trim-screen-recording-mac) has the steps.
- **Shrink it before sharing.** Mac recordings can be large. [Making a screen recording smaller](/blog/reduce-screen-recording-file-size-mac) covers exporting at a lower resolution, and the [file size calculator](/tools/screen-recording-file-size-calculator) estimates sizes before you record.
- **Check for private details.** Notifications, open tabs and messages can slip into a recording. Turn on Do Not Disturb before you start.
