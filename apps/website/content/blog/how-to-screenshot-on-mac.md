To take a screenshot on a Mac, press **Shift-Command-3** to capture the whole screen, or **Shift-Command-4** and drag to capture part of it. Press **Shift-Command-5** to open the Screenshot app, which adds window capture, a timer, screen recording and a choice of where to save. Screenshots land on your desktop unless you change it.

Below is every method, step by step, plus how to copy instead of save and how to hide private details before you share.

## How do I screenshot the whole screen?

1. Press **Shift-Command-3** together.
2. A thumbnail appears in the corner of the screen. Click it to mark up the screenshot, or wait and it saves to the desktop.

With more than one display, Shift-Command-3 saves a separate image of each screen.

## How do I screenshot part of the screen?

1. Press **Shift-Command-4**. The pointer turns into a crosshair.
2. Drag over the area you want. Hold **Space** while dragging to move the selection without resizing it.
3. Let go to capture, or press **Esc** to cancel.

This is the shortcut most people use day to day, because it captures only what matters and keeps the file small.

## How do I screenshot a single window?

1. Open the window or menu you want.
2. Press **Shift-Command-4**, then press **Space**. The pointer becomes a camera.
3. Click the window. Hold **Option** while you click to leave out the window's shadow.

The shadow-free version is useful for documentation, where a shadow adds a transparent border around the image.

## How do I use the Screenshot app?

Press **Shift-Command-5**, or open **Screenshot** from **Applications → Utilities**. A toolbar appears with buttons to capture the entire screen, a window or a selected portion, and to record the entire screen or a portion of it.

The **Options** menu in that toolbar controls:

- **Save to:** the desktop, Documents, the clipboard, or an app such as Mail or Preview.
- **Timer:** a 5 or 10 second delay, handy for capturing menus and hover states.
- **Show Floating Thumbnail:** turn off the corner preview if it gets in the way.
- **Show Mouse Pointer:** include or hide the pointer in captures.

Whatever you choose stays set for the next screenshot. For recording, see our [screen recording guides](/blog/topics/how-to/).

## How do I copy a screenshot to the clipboard?

Add **Control** to any of the shortcuts:

| Shortcut | Result |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>Shift</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>3</kbd> | Whole screen to the clipboard |
| <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>Shift</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>4</kbd> | Selected area to the clipboard |
| <kbd>Control</kbd>-<kbd>Shift</kbd>-<kbd>Command</kbd>-<kbd>4</kbd>, then <kbd>Space</kbd> | A window to the clipboard |

Then paste with **Command-V** into a chat, email or document. No file is left on your desktop. If you forget which is which, the free [screenshot shortcut finder](/tools/mac-screenshot-shortcuts) gives you the exact keys in two clicks.

## How do I hide private information in a screenshot?

The Markup tools that open from the thumbnail can draw shapes and text, but they have no blur or pixelate. A shape drawn over a password can also sometimes be removed later if the image keeps layers. Two safer options:

- The free [browser screenshot editor](/editor) on this site blurs, pixelates or blocks out any area and writes the change into the exported pixels. Your image never leaves your browser.
- The [Screenmac](/download) Mac app does the same right after each capture, with its own shortcuts (**Option-Shift-Command-4** for an area) that do not clash with the built-in ones.

## Which method should I use?

| You want to | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Capture everything on screen | Shift-Command-3 |
| Capture just one part | Shift-Command-4 |
| Capture a window cleanly | Shift-Command-4, Space, Option-click |
| Paste a screenshot straight into chat | Add Control to the shortcut |
| Capture a menu or hover state | Shift-Command-5 with the timer |
| Blur or redact before sharing | The browser editor or Screenmac |
